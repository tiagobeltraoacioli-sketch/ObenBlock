# OBIP-3: Certified Bloch anchors

```

  Layer: Consensus (hard fork)
  Title: Certified Bloch anchors
  Author: gnb-smh <gnb-smh@proton.me>
  Status: Draft
  Type: Standards Track
  Created: 2026-10-18
  License: BSD-2-Clause
  Requires: none
```

## Abstract

The base anchors of ObenBlock checkpoints in Bloch (`DESIGN-obenblock.md` §8,
phase 1) are advisory: a record carries no proof that the witness
configuration finalized it, anyone with a Bloch account can publish first,
and a node only raises an alert when an anchor contradicts it. This OBIP
upgrades them to **certified anchors**. A record names the PoCI configuration
and carries a commitment to a finality certificate (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md`
§6) or a compact certificate (OBIP-2); the Bloch anchor registry verifies the
certificate before accepting the record. To let a verifier that is not a full
ObenBlock node follow configuration handoffs, every checkpoint block commits
the identifier of the configuration its history produces (a consensus hard
fork). Nodes may then use certified anchors automatically as
weak-subjectivity checkpoints when they bootstrap, while their own sticky
finality keeps priority. The Bloch side needs access to the Bloch code base.

## Motivation

- **The advisory gap.** `DESIGN-obenblock.md` §8 states that automatic use
  "needs a finality certificate in the record (≥ 2/3 of W signing, or a
  succinct proof of it), checked by the node against the W of the candidate
  chain". `DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §6 states that anchors stay advisory
  "until the anchor registry verifies a certificate; then the record carries
  the certificate hash and the registry checks it, instead of 'first record
  wins'".
- **Bootstrap.** PoCI's launch (§9) and every later sync depend on a
  weak-subjectivity checkpoint shipped in a release. A new node, or one that
  returns after a long time, needs a recent authenticated checkpoint; work and
  KES alone do not give objective bootstrap in every scenario (technical note,
  "Bloch e recuperação"; Ethereum, "Weak subjectivity").
- **Configuration lineage is not light-verifiable today.** A certificate
  proves a link under `config_id`. It does not say what the next
  configuration is: `C_{e+k}` is computed from finalized history
  (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §4), which only a full node has. A registry on
  Bloch, or a bootstrapping node, cannot move its trust from `C_e` to the next
  configuration without a committed value.

## Specification

### 1. Configuration commitment (consensus, hard fork)

From the activation height, every block at a checkpoint slot commits

```text
cfg_commit(B) = config_id of C_{epoch(B)} computed from B's history by the
                handoff rule of DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §4 (weights, growth
                cap §6.3 of DESIGN-obenblock.md, pruning §4.1, exits, bans)
```

- A full node recomputes `cfg_commit(B)` when it validates `B` and rejects the
  block if the committed value differs.
- When `B` is finalized under `C_e` with `epoch(B) > e`, the handoff makes
  `C_{epoch(B)}` the configuration in force, and its `config_id` equals
  `cfg_commit(B)`. A verifier holding a trusted `C_e`, a certificate finalizing
  `B` under `C_e`, the 80-byte header of `B`, the commitment carrier and its
  Merkle branch to `merkle_root` therefore learns the next trusted
  `config_id`.
- OPEN: the carrier. Options: (a) a coinbase output with a fixed tag, e.g.
  `"OBB-CFG1" ‖ config_id`, proved by the coinbase Merkle branch; (b) a field
  of a new attestation message that commits `next_config_id` for links whose
  source opens an epoch. (a) leaves the vote format unchanged; (b) needs no
  block-body proof but changes `msg_v3` and the evidence rules.
- OPEN: the exact history `cfg_commit` is computed from. The base handoff
  computes `C_x` from the finalized history up to the finalizing checkpoint;
  the commitment must equal that value byte for byte for every checkpoint
  that can trigger a handoff, including skipped epochs and blocks of earlier
  epochs merged late by GhostDAG. The reference model fixes this before the
  OBIP leaves Draft.
- OPEN: the cost of recomputing a configuration at every checkpoint (every
  60 blocks), and whether the commitment is required at every checkpoint or
  only at the first checkpoint of an epoch.

### 2. Lineage proof

```text
handoff_step = certificate (full, DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §6, or compact, OBIP-2)
             ‖ header(B)[80] ‖ carrier proof (per the OPEN choice above)
lineage      = sequence of handoff_step, each under the configuration the
               previous step established
```

Starting from a trusted root configuration, a verifier applies each step:
the certificate must verify under the current trusted `config_id`, its source
must be `B`, `epoch(B)` must exceed the current configuration's epoch, and
the step's carrier must give `cfg_commit(B)`, which becomes the trusted
`config_id`.

- Root of trust: `C_4`, named by the launch release (PoCI §9), or a later
  `config_id` shipped in a release.

### 3. Anchor record version 2

The base record is `chain_id ‖ slot ‖ block_id ‖ finalized_at ‖ W-digest`
(`DESIGN-obenblock.md` §8). Version 2:

```text
record_v2 = u8(version = 2)
          ‖ u32le(chain_id)
          ‖ u64le(slot)                 height of the finalized checkpoint
          ‖ block_id[32]
          ‖ u64le(finalized_at)         target height of the finalizing link
          ‖ u64le(config_epoch)
          ‖ config_id[32]
          ‖ u8(cert_kind)               1 = full certificate, 2 = compact (OBIP-2)
          ‖ cert_commitment[32]

cert_commitment = SHA-256d("OBB-OBIP3/cert" ‖ certificate wire bytes)   cert_kind 1
                = statement_id (OBIP-2)                                  cert_kind 2
```

- The record is published, as in the base, for every finalized checkpoint
  that starts a new epoch, by any operator through the reference
  `obenblock-anchor` daemon.
- The certificate itself, and any lineage steps the registry does not yet
  have, accompany the submission. OPEN: whether the registry stores the
  certificate bytes or only their commitment after verification.

### 4. Bloch anchor registry (Bloch side)

The registry keeps, per ObenBlock `chain_id`, the trusted `config_id` and
epoch, the last accepted slot and a status (`ok` or `conflict`). It accepts a
submission only if:

1. `chain_id` is registered, and the status is `ok`;
2. any lineage steps in the submission verify (§2) and advance the trusted
   configuration;
3. `config_id` of the record equals the trusted one;
4. the certificate verifies under it and finalizes `block_id` at `slot`
   (source `slot`, target `finalized_at = slot + checkpoint_interval`);
5. `slot` is greater than the last accepted slot, or equal to an accepted
   record's slot (case 6).
6. A second submission with a valid certificate for an already accepted
   `slot` and a different `block_id` is proof that two conflicting checkpoints
   were certified: the registry stores both, sets `conflict` and accepts no
   further records for that chain until a governance action defined in
   advance. OPEN: that action on Bloch.

- The registry never accepts a v2 record by "first record wins" alone; the
  certificate decides. Version 1 records may still be published and stay
  advisory.
- OPEN: how the registry verifies certificates. A full certificate is about
  `K × 8.7 kB` of hybrid post-quantum signatures (OBIP-2, Motivation), which
  may be impractical for Bloch transactions; a compact certificate requires an
  accepted post-quantum `proof_system` of OBIP-2. Groth16 (`0x0001`) must not
  be accepted by the registry.
- OPEN: ancestry. The registry checks one record per slot and monotone slots;
  it does not check that a new checkpoint descends from the previous one
  (a GhostDAG ancestry proof is not specified). Under PoCI a certified
  non-descendant at a later slot also implies more than a third of a
  configuration signed conflicting links, but the registry would not detect
  it by itself.
- *Pending access to the Bloch repository* (`DESIGN-obenblock.md` §10, open
  point 1).

### 5. Node use

- A node reads records through `--bloch-rpc URL` as in the base, or through
  the Bloch light client of `DESIGN-obenblock.md` §8 phase 2 (OBIP-4).
- A node itself verifies the certificate and the lineage from its release
  root of trust; the registry's verdict is not trusted on its own.
- **Bootstrap.** A node with no finality of its own above the release
  checkpoint may adopt the newest record that is (a) Bloch-finalized, (b)
  verified by the node itself, and (c) not older than the release
  checkpoint, as its finality checkpoint, with the effect of
  `setfinalitycheckpoint`.
- **Running node.** A certified anchor that agrees with the node's finality is
  reported as corroboration. One that the node cannot place (unknown block)
  makes the node fetch that block and its ancestors. One that contradicts the
  node's own finality never moves the node's sticky pin: the node raises the
  conflict alert (`getfinalityinfo`) as in the base, and the operator decides.
  Two verified conflicting certificates are also a PoCI corruption case
  (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §11); the node keeps both.
- Records without a certificate (v1, or v2 failing verification) are
  advisory, exactly as in the base.
- OPEN: the option name and default (`--anchor-checkpoints=auto|advisory|off`),
  and whether `auto` is the default.

## Rationale

- **The certificate decides, Bloch orders and publishes.** A node verifies the
  ObenBlock certificate itself, so the automatic use rests on PoCI's quorum,
  not on Bloch's honesty. Bloch adds a public, timestamped, single record per
  slot, a public conflict flag and an availability point for bootstrapping
  nodes.
- **Why a configuration commitment.** Without it the registry would have to
  trust the submitter for the next configuration, which is the "first record
  wins" weakness under another name.
- **Rejected: self-anchoring.** Recording ObenBlock checkpoints in ObenBlock
  itself proves nothing to a node that is being fed a different chain.
- **Rejected: merged mining** (with Bitcoin or Bloch) as the protection: a
  base decision (`DESIGN-obenblock.md` §1), and it ties ObenBlock security to
  another chain's miners without a finality statement.
- **Rejected: stake bond on Bloch** for anchor submitters: ObenBlock weight is
  share work only (base decision); the certificate, not a deposit, is what
  makes a record trustworthy.
- **Not proposed: Bloch as recovery authority.** Letting Bloch authorize a new
  configuration after PoCI loses its quorum (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §7)
  is possible only if that power and its limits are defined in advance; it is
  an explicit trust dependency, not autonomous BFT (technical note, "Bloch e
  recuperação"). It would be a separate OBIP.
- **Not proposed: settlement conditioned on Bloch.** Requiring a finalized
  Bloch record before crediting puts Bloch's latency and availability on the
  critical path of every payment. Applications may choose it; this OBIP does
  not require it.

## Backwards Compatibility

- §1 is a hard fork: from the activation height, checkpoint blocks without a
  correct commitment are invalid. Activation is a coordinated ObenBlock
  release height at an epoch boundary (OBIP-1), regtest first. Below the
  height every verdict is unchanged.
- Version 1 anchor records remain valid on Bloch and advisory on nodes.
- The Bloch-to-ObenBlock direction (`docs/BLOCH-ANCHOR.md`, BLA1 transactions)
  is unchanged.
- GnB is unaffected.

## Security Considerations

- **Assumptions.** Less than a third of each configuration in the lineage is
  Byzantine; the certificate (and, for compact certificates, the proof
  system and circuit of OBIP-2) is sound; the release root of trust is
  correct. The commitment of §1 is certified because more than two thirds of
  `C_e` finalized a block that full nodes reject when the commitment is
  wrong; if more than two thirds of `C_e` are dishonest, the lineage can be
  forged from that point, which is the base safety bound.
- **Long-range and stale anchors.** A node can be shown an old, valid lineage
  by an attacker who eclipses it. KES erasure (`DESIGN-powit-kes.md`) keeps
  honest past keys from signing past epochs, but keys that are compromised or
  sold while they still hold weight can. The node must prefer the newest
  Bloch-finalized record and must not accept a checkpoint older than its
  release root. OPEN: the weak-subjectivity period after which a node refuses
  to bootstrap without a fresh release or anchor.
- **Bloch trust.** With `--bloch-rpc`, the node trusts that RPC for which
  records exist and are Bloch-finalized (not for their validity, which it
  checks). A withheld record delays bootstrap; it cannot make the node accept
  a wrong checkpoint. The Bloch light client of phase 2 (OBIP-4) removes the RPC trust.
- **Post-quantum status.** Full certificates use hybrid ML-DSA-65 +
  Falcon-1024 signatures (about 4.6 kB each). Compact certificates are
  post-quantum only with a hash-based proof system (STARK proofs are tens to
  hundreds of kB); pairing SNARKs, BLS and Groth16 are not post-quantum and
  are not accepted. Bloch's own consensus and signature assumptions apply to
  the ordering and availability of records.
- **Registry DoS.** Certificate verification on Bloch must be bounded and
  paid by the submitter. OPEN: limits.
- **Conflict freeze.** A correct conflict flag stops the registry for the
  chain, which is the intended behavior, but it gives anyone who obtains two
  valid conflicting certificates (that is, more than a third of a
  configuration equivocating) the ability to freeze it.

## Deployment prerequisites

- Access to the Bloch repository for the registry (`DESIGN-obenblock.md`
  §10).
- Either a registry able to verify full certificates within Bloch's limits,
  or OBIP-2 Final with an accepted post-quantum `proof_system`.
- The `cfg_commit` rule in the PoCI reference model, with vectors, and the
  carrier decision.
- Partition and stall simulations re-run with the commitment, including the
  open issues of the partition simulator (`obenblock/o11b-partition-sim`,
  `docs/research/obenblock-partition-sim.md`): a thin configuration after a
  stall longer than the window, and the losing side of a long partition
  leaving the configuration. A certified anchor makes such a configuration
  the trusted one for bootstrapping nodes.
- The three-lens review of OBIP-1.
