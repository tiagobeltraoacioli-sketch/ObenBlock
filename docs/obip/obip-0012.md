## Abstract

PoCI (DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md) finalizes checkpoints every 60 blocks with
more than two thirds of the whole configuration `C_e`, and inclusion alone
needs `ceil(K/32)` blocks (CONSENSUS.md "PoWit", "Safety and liveness").
This Informational OBIP records research on a faster, weaker signal: a
**per-height committee certificate**. For each selected-chain height a
committee of about `n >= 128` seats is drawn from `C_e` in proportion to
weight by secret self-selection (a post-quantum, hash-based lottery is
sketched; classical VRFs are not post-quantum). Seats sign the block they
see at that height; more than `2n/3` seat votes form an asynchronous
certificate that never blocks block production. The OBIP gives binomial
tables for capture and stall, the bandwidth of post-quantum certificates,
and the argument why a committee must never be recomposed in rounds unless
the rounds are ordered by one history all nodes share. It does not claim the
construction is safe: in particular, a fast certificate implies nothing
about PoCI finality unless fork choice is changed to respect it, which is
the main open question. No rule is proposed for activation.

## Motivation

- A PoCI checkpoint interval is 60 blocks (about 30 minutes at the 30 s
  target, DESIGN-obenblock.md §4.2), and justification needs `ceil(K/32)`
  blocks of inclusion for the `K` heaviest keys holding two thirds.
  Payments that cannot wait for that latency use provisional confirmations
  today.
- The technical note behind PoCI (P1, section on changing voters) asks
  whether sampling a smaller set of voters could give faster decisions, and
  shows that a sample trades the guarantee over all the weight for a
  statistical one, with risks multiplied by every new draw.
- This document fixes what such a sample can and cannot give, so that any
  later Standards Track proposal starts from stated numbers and from the
  rules already rejected for the base.

## Specification

This section describes a candidate design for research. Nothing here is a
consensus rule. Every value marked OPEN is undecided.

### Scope and meaning

- A **fast certificate** for height `h` names one block `B` with
  `height(B) = h` on the signer's selected chain, under the configuration
  `C_e` in force for `epoch(h)` (DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §2, §4).
- It is **not** PoCI finality. PoCI remains the only finality; certificates
  under PoCI (§6) are unchanged.
- **Never blocking.** Block production, GhostDAG and PoCI never wait for a
  fast certificate. A certificate may form at any time after `B` (it is
  asynchronous: there are no rounds and no timeouts), or never.

### Committee

- **Seed.** `seed_e = SHA-256d("OBB-FAST-v0/seed" ‖ u32le(chain_id) ‖
  config_id(C_e) ‖ handoff_checkpoint_e.hash)`, where `handoff_checkpoint_e`
  is the checkpoint whose finalization put `C_e` into force
  (DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §4). Under PoCI's safety assumption it is unique,
  so every honest node derives the same seed for the whole epoch.
- **Seats per height.** For each height `h` of epoch `e`, a committee of
  expected size `n` is drawn from `entries_e` in proportion to weight, with
  replacement: a key may hold several seats and votes with all of them.
- **Secret self-selection (candidate, post-quantum).** Each key commits, in
  finalized history before `C_e` is computed, to a hash chain
  `L_k = H^N(s_k)` with `N` = the heights of one epoch (720). For the `i`-th
  height of the epoch the key reveals `r = H^(N-i)(s_k)`; anyone checks
  `H^i(r) = L_k`. Its seat count is a deterministic function of
  `u = SHA-256d("OBB-FAST-v0/seat" ‖ seed_e ‖ u64le(h) ‖ key_id ‖ r)` and
  `weight_k / total_e` (binomial inverse-CDF sortition as in Algorand,
  technical note R15). `r` is unique given `L_k`, so the key cannot grind
  its seats, and it stays secret until the key reveals it.
  - OPEN: where `L_k` is registered (a new field of the configuration leaf
    changes `merkle_root` and `config_id`, DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §2).
  - OPEN: the sortition function; with self-selection the committee size is
    random, so thresholds must be set against its distribution, not against
    a fixed `n` (the tables below assume a fixed `n`).
- Classical VRFs (ECVRF and similar) are excluded on the mainnet path: they
  are not post-quantum. ML-DSA and Falcon signatures are randomized and so
  not unique; using them as lottery tickets would allow grinding.

### Fast vote and certificate (candidate encodings)

```text
msg_fast = SHA3-256( u32le(16) ‖ "OBB-FAST-v0/vote" ‖ u32le(chain_id)
                     ‖ config_id[32] ‖ u8(depth) ‖ root[32]
                     ‖ u64le(h) ‖ block_id[32] ‖ r[32] )
```

signed by the KES leaf of `epoch(h)` (DESIGN-powit-kes.md), committing the
KES public key as `msg_v3` does (DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §2). A certificate
for `(h, block_id)` is a set of fast votes of distinct keys whose seat counts
sum to more than `2n/3` (OPEN: the threshold for random committee size).

- **One vote per height, ever.** A signer votes at most once per `(config_id,
  h)`, persisted before release as in DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §8, even if its
  selected chain later reorgs to another block at `h`.
- **Evidence.** Two fast votes of one key for one `(config_id, h)` and
  different `block_id` are attributable. OPEN: whether they are Proof of
  Corruption under DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §5 (100-epoch ban) or only
  reported.
- **Carriage.** Certificates do not fit in block bodies (32 attestations per
  block, CONSENSUS.md "PoWit"). They travel as peer-services objects; OBIP-5
  (out-of-body vote aggregation) and OBIP-2 (compact post-quantum finality
  certificates) are the related work for carrying and compressing them.

### Relation to fork choice (the open problem)

Without a fork-choice change, the honest selected chain can move away from a
fast-certified block (a heavier DAG branch arrives) and PoCI then finalizes
the other branch, **with no equivocation by anyone**. A fast certificate
alone is therefore not even a probabilistic finality guarantee. Options:

| Option | Effect | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Advisory only | wallets display "committee-confirmed at h" | no guarantee beyond "the committee saw B"; reorgs remain possible |
| B. Fork choice prefers fast-certified blocks | a certificate becomes binding on the selected chain | conflicting certificates (adversary > n/3 of seats) split nodes; needs a deterministic rule that cannot be gamed and a safety proof together with PoCI |
| C. PoCI duty votes only for checkpoints consistent with fast certificates | makes certificates binding on finality | lowers PoCI's safety to the committee's statistical bound; a conflicting pair could stall PoCI |

OPEN: no option is recommended for mainnet. Option A is the only one that
leaves the base untouched; B and C are consensus changes that need a formal
model and the partition simulator (`reference/sim/partition_sim.py`, branch
`obenblock/o11b-partition-sim`).

### Capture and stall: binomial tables

Model (from the technical note, P1): `n` independent seats, each drawn in
proportion to weight; an adversary (or, for stall, adversary plus offline
weight) holding fraction `p` of `total_e`; static corruption for the epoch;
one draw per block. The number of adversarial seats is `X ~ Bin(n, p)`.

- **Capture (conflicting certificates possible):** `X > n/3`.
- **Stall (no certificate at that height):** `X >= n/3`, so the honest seats
  cannot exceed `2n/3`. For the `n` below (not multiples of 3) both events
  are `X >= floor(n/3) + 1`.
- **Sole control (adversary certifies alone):** `X > 2n/3`.
- **Per year:** union bound `R × P`, `R = 1,051,200` draws (30 s blocks),
  capped at 1. It is a bound, not a measured fork probability.

Computed with exact binomial tails in log space (own computation; it
reproduces the note's 2.759 %, 1.70e-9 and 2.02e-32 for `n` = 100, 1,000,
4,000 at `p` = 25 %).

Read as a **stall** table (`p` = adversarial plus offline weight), the same
column gives the probability that a height gets no certificate: with 30 %
of the weight offline and `n = 128`, about one height in five has none.
That is acceptable only because a missing certificate costs nothing (PoCI
continues).

Conclusions under this model:

- `n = 128` gives a useful yearly bound only for `p` around 10 %.
- For `p = 25 %`, a yearly bound below 1e-10 needs `n` of about 2,048.
- No `n` in the table gives a useful bound at `p = 30 %`. The full-weight
  quorum of PoCI has no such statistical term.
- The model ignores sampling without replacement, correlated weights,
  biased seeds and adaptive corruption; each needs its own model.

### Bandwidth of post-quantum certificates

Per vote, from DESIGN-powit-kes.md: KES public key about 3.7 KB, signature
about 4.6 KB (one hybrid ML-DSA-65 + Falcon-1024 signature), path 384 B:
about 8.7 kB, or about 5.0 kB if public keys are cached. Votes needed:
`floor(2n/3) + 1`.

| n | votes | certificate, about | per day (2,880 blocks), about |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 128 | 86 | 750 kB (430 kB with cached keys) | 2.2 GB |
| 256 | 171 | 1.5 MB (0.86 MB) | 4.3 GB |
| 512 | 342 | 3.0 MB (1.7 MB) | 8.6 GB |
| 1024 | 683 | 5.9 MB (3.4 MB) | 17 GB |

Gossip of all seat votes, not only the certificate, adds to this. The block
size limit is 1,000,000 bytes (CONSENSUS.md "Block size"). A compact proof
of the certificate on a post-quantum path (STARK family) is expected to be
tens to hundreds of kB and is research (OBIP-2); pairing-based aggregation
(BLS) or SNARKs (Groth16) would be small but are not post-quantum.

### Why multi-round recomposition needs one external history

A tempting extension is: if a committee does not certify in time, draw a new
committee for the same height (round `r + 1`). The base rejected it
(DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §1 and §4: no local timeout changes who counts),
and the reason applies here:

1. **Quorum intersection holds inside one committee.** Two certificates of
   more than `2n/3` from the same seats share more than `n/3` seats. Two
   certificates from different committees (round 1 and round 2) share no
   seats by construction, so honest seats in the intersection cannot stop a
   conflict.
2. **"Round 1 failed" is a local observation.** Under a partition, side A
   sees round 1 time out and moves to round 2 while side B certifies in
   round 1 (or also times out and draws its own round 2). Each side's
   certificate is valid against its own round; nobody signed twice.
3. **Locks do not fix it under partition.** Tendermint and HotStuff carry
   locks across rounds because every honest validator of one fixed set sees
   prior-round votes eventually (technical note R8, R9). A recomposed
   committee's new seats never voted in the earlier round and cannot be
   locked by it; importing a lock rule into a different family does not
   transfer its proof.
4. **Every round is another draw.** If an adversary can force timeouts
   (withholding its seats is enough), it gets more samples per height: the
   union bound grows with the number of rounds `R` it can cause.

The only safe way to change the committee for one decision is for the event
"round `r` ended" to be ordered in one history that every honest node
agrees on before it acts on round `r + 1`. On ObenBlock that history is PoCI
finality itself, which is exactly the slow path this OBIP tries to
accelerate; or an external history (for example Bloch finality), which makes
that chain a trust and liveness dependency of ObenBlock's consensus, the
external-anchor dependency the base kept advisory (DESIGN-obenblock.md §8,
BLOCH-ANCHOR.md). Hence this design has one committee per `(epoch, height)`,
no rounds, and "no certificate" as the failure mode.

## Rationale

- **Full-weight PoCI stays the finality.** Its quorum has no sampling term;
  a committee is at best an additional, weaker signal.
- **Seed from the handoff checkpoint.** It is the one value every honest
  node agrees on for the epoch under PoCI. A seed from the block itself, or
  from a proof's free bytes or a signature list, could be varied by its
  producer to obtain several draws (technical note, P1).
- **Local committee change** (replacing absent seats by whoever answers)
  rewards fast networks and lets an attacker who cuts honest seats off pick
  the committee; rejected for the base and here.
- **Random multi-round recomposition**: rejected, see above.
- **Blind voting and receipt-free voting**: hiding which key voted would
  defeat attributable evidence (DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md §5) and weight
  verification; secret self-selection hides seats only until the vote.
- **Stake bond** for committee members: rejected for the base (share work
  only, DESIGN-obenblock.md §1); without it, misbehaviour costs exclusion
  and lost share rewards, not a deposit.
- **Merged mining** to borrow another chain's committee: rejected for the
  base (DESIGN-obenblock.md §1).
- **Anchoring** fast certificates in Bloch: adds Bloch as a dependency
  without verifying the certificate (anchors are advisory, §8).

## Security Considerations

- The tables assume static corruption over an epoch and independent
  weight-proportional seats. Secret self-selection protects against
  corruption targeted before a vote, not after: a seat that has revealed and
  voted is known.
- Seed bias: the miner of the checkpoint block that becomes
  `handoff_checkpoint_e` can withhold or try several blocks, at the cost of
  their proof of work. OPEN: whether that bias needs a VDF (technical note
  R14) or a combination of reveals.
- Hash-chain reveals are post-quantum under the hash function; KES votes are
  hybrid ML-DSA-65 + Falcon-1024 (about 4.6 kB per signature). Pairing
  SNARKs, BLS aggregation and Groth16 are not post-quantum; STARK proofs are
  tens to hundreds of kB.
- A fast certificate under option A gives no finality guarantee (see
  "Relation to fork choice"). Wallets must keep crediting at PoCI finality
  (DESIGN-obenblock.md §7) for anything that matters.
- Equivocation between fast votes is attributable but, without stake, the
  only sanctions are exclusion and lost share rewards.

## Deployment prerequisites

- A formal model of the chosen fork-choice option together with PoCI, run
  through the partition simulator with committee-level adversaries.
- A post-quantum sortition with a stated distribution of committee size and
  thresholds derived from it.
- Measured bandwidth of votes and certificates, with OBIP-5 and OBIP-2.
- A decision on whether fast-vote equivocation is Proof of Corruption.
