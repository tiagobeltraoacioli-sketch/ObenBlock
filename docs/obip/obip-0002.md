## Abstract

A PoCI finality certificate (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §6) carries every
vote of a supermajority: each vote holds a KES public key and a hybrid
ML-DSA-65 + Falcon-1024 signature with its leaf key and path. That is close to
9 kB per voter, which makes the certificate too large for light clients, for
the Bloch anchor registry (OBIP-3) and for any other verifier that is not a
full ObenBlock node. This OBIP defines a **compact certificate**: the public
statement of a PoCI certificate (chain, configuration, link, weights, signer
set), plus a succinct proof that a full certificate satisfying that statement
exists and verifies under the base rules. The target is a hash-based
(STARK-family) proof with a post-quantum security assumption. A pairing-based
SNARK (Groth16) is kept only as a laboratory baseline for comparison. Size,
prover-time and verify-time targets are open until a reproducible prototype
is measured. The compact certificate never replaces the full certificate
inside ObenBlock consensus and is never evidence of corruption.

## Motivation

- **Size of the base certificate.** Per vote, the base wire encoding
  (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §6) carries `u8(depth) ‖ root[32]` and a
  `KesSignature`: the leaf public key (about 3.7 kB), the hybrid signature
  (about 4.6 kB) and the 12 × 32-byte path (`DESIGN-powit-kes.md`,
  "Construction"). That is about 8.7 kB per voter, before the registry proof
  (weight, index, Merkle siblings). With `K` the smallest number of keys
  holding more than two thirds of the configuration, a certificate is about
  `K × 8.7 kB`: about 870 kB for `K = 100`, about 16.7 MB for `K = 1,920`
  (the largest `K` whose votes still fit in one checkpoint interval at 32
  attestations per block, `CONSENSUS.md` "PoWit", "Safety and liveness").
- **Verification cost.** A verifier must check `K` hybrid signatures and `K`
  Merkle paths. That is acceptable for a full node, which already does it as
  votes are included, but not for a contract or registry on another chain,
  a phone wallet, or a node that bootstraps from an anchor.
- **Consumers.** Certified Bloch anchors (OBIP-3), weak-subjectivity
  bootstrap, bridges and light clients all need to check "more than two
  thirds of configuration `C` finalized this link" without the full vote set.
- The technical note "Votes on shares, compact proofs and safe
  finality" (2026-10-18) lists this as P1/P2 work: a prototype of the proof
  relation, two laboratory tracks (a classical succinct baseline and a
  PQ-compatible option, without confusing their guarantees), and an
  aggregated certificate with replaceable aggregators.

## Specification

### Statement

The public statement of a compact certificate:

```text
statement = u8(version = 1)
          ‖ u32le(chain_id)
          ‖ config_id[32]
          ‖ u64le(source.height) ‖ source.hash[32]
          ‖ u64le(target.height) ‖ target.hash[32]
          ‖ u128le(total)                      total weight of the configuration
          ‖ u128le(signed)                     sum of the signers' registry weights
          ‖ u32le(n)                           number of entries of the configuration
          ‖ signers_digest[32]

signers_bitmap = n bits, bit i set iff entry i (in registry order) signed,
                 packed little-endian within each byte, ceil(n/8) bytes,
                 unused high bits of the last byte zero
signers_digest = SHA-256d("OBB-OBIP2/signers" ‖ u32le(n) ‖ signers_bitmap)
statement_id   = SHA-256d("OBB-OBIP2/statement" ‖ statement)
```

- `config_id`, the link fields and `chain_id` have the meaning and encoding of
  `DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §2 and §6 (as updated with exact encodings on
  `obenblock/o11a-poci-reference`).
- The **identity** of a compact certificate is `statement_id`. Proof bytes are
  not an identity and must never be hashed into a seed, an index or a
  uniqueness key: several valid proofs of one statement can exist.

### Relation

A proof for `statement` shows knowledge of a witness

```text
witness = ( epoch, params_digest, tree_root,
            for each signer i (ascending key_id): weight_i, index_i, path_i,
                                                   depth_i, root_i, kes_signature_i )
```

such that all of the base rules for a valid certificate hold
(`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §2, §3, §6):

1. `config_id = SHA-256d("OBB-POCI-v1/config" ‖ u32le(chain_id) ‖ u64le(epoch)
   ‖ u128le(total) ‖ merkle_root ‖ params_digest)`, with
   `merkle_root = tagged("OBB-POCI-v1/root", u32le(n) ‖ tree_root)`.
2. For each signer, `key_id_i = witness_id(depth_i, root_i)`, the leaf
   `leaf(key_id_i, weight_i)` reaches `tree_root` through `path_i` at
   `index_i` (shape fixed by `(index_i, n)`), and `weight_i > 0`.
3. The `key_id_i` are strictly ascending (no key twice), and bit `index_i` of
   `signers_bitmap` is set exactly for the signers.
4. `kes::verify((depth_i, root_i), epoch(target.height), msg_v3,
   kes_signature_i)` accepts, where `msg_v3` is the base attestation message
   v3 over `chain_id`, `config_id`, `(depth_i, root_i)`, `source`, `target`.
   This includes the canonical hybrid ML-DSA-65 ‖ Falcon-1024 verification and
   the SHA3 KES path.
5. The link is a checkpoint link: both heights are slots and
   `target.height = source.height + checkpoint_interval`, with
   `checkpoint_interval` as committed in `params_digest`.
6. `signed = sum(weight_i)` exactly (u128, no saturation) and
   `3 × signed > 2 × total` (the strict quorum, `DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md`
   §3).

OPEN: whether the relation is proved monolithically, per vote then
aggregated (recursion or folding), or per batch of votes. The choice is a
prototype result, not a specification input.

### Wire encoding

```text
compact_certificate = statement
                    ‖ u16le(proof_system)
                    ‖ vk_digest[32]
                    ‖ varint(ceil(n/8)) ‖ signers_bitmap
                    ‖ varint(len) ‖ proof
```

- `varint` is the repository's minimal varint; non-minimal encodings,
  truncation and trailing bytes are refused.
- `vk_digest` names the exact verifying key or circuit (and its parameters).
  A verifier accepts only `(proof_system, vk_digest)` pairs it was configured
  with; a certificate naming an unknown pair is refused, not "best effort"
  verified.
- `proof_system` registry:

  | id | system | status |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `0x0000` | reserved | never valid |
  | `0x0001` | Groth16 (pairing-based), laboratory baseline | **not post-quantum; must never be accepted where a verdict has security value** (OBIP-3 registry, automatic checkpoints, bridges) |
  | `0x0002` | hash-based transparent proof (STARK family) | OPEN: concrete system, field, hash, FRI/commitment parameters, conjectured security level in bits |

- OPEN: `MAX_COMPACT_CERT_BYTES`, the size cap a verifier enforces before
  any work.

### Verification

In order, first failure wins:

1. Decode (version, truncation, minimal varints, trailing bytes).
2. `chain_id` equals the verifier's.
3. `config_id` is one the verifier trusts (its own chain's, or reached by a
   lineage it has verified; OBIP-3 specifies one lineage). A certificate under
   a configuration its signers made up proves nothing
   (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §6).
4. `total` and `n` equal those of the trusted configuration, when the verifier
   knows them.
5. Link shape (slots, `target = source + checkpoint_interval`).
6. `3 × signed > 2 × total` on the public values.
7. Bitmap length is `ceil(n/8)`, unused bits are zero, and
   `signers_digest` matches.
8. `(proof_system, vk_digest)` is accepted by the verifier's configuration.
9. The proof verifies against `statement`.

### Serving

- RPC: `getcompactcertificate <checkpoint> [proof_system]` returns the
  compact certificate if the node has or can build one. OPEN: whether nodes
  build proofs themselves or only relay proofs produced by aggregators.
- Aggregators: anyone holding the full certificate may produce a proof. No
  aggregator is designated; a missing aggregator must not stall finality,
  because ObenBlock finality itself never waits for a compact certificate.
- OPEN: a P2P message for relaying compact certificates, and its DoS limits.

### What it is not

- It is not a consensus object. Blocks keep carrying individual attestations
  (`CONSENSUS.md` "PoWit"), full nodes keep the full certificates
  (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §6), and fork choice and finality do not read
  compact certificates. Replacing included votes by an aggregate to lift the
  `ceil(K/32)` inclusion bound would be a separate Consensus OBIP.
- It is not evidence. Proof of Corruption (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §5)
  needs the two signed votes. Two conflicting compact certificates of one
  configuration show, through their bitmaps, which entries signed both
  (more than a third of the weight, by the quorum arithmetic), so operators
  know whose votes to fetch from full nodes, but the bitmaps themselves ban
  nobody.

## Rationale

- **Why a proof and not signature aggregation.** The base signatures are
  hybrid ML-DSA-65 + Falcon-1024 inside a KES tree. Neither scheme has a
  standard non-interactive aggregation; a succinct proof of verification is
  the general way to compress them.
- **Why hash-based.** Pairing-based SNARKs (Groth16, KZG-based systems) and
  BLS aggregation rest on pairing-friendly elliptic curves and are not
  post-quantum. Wrapping a post-quantum signature inside a classical SNARK
  makes the SNARK's soundness part of the vote's security: the inner
  post-quantum content does not make the outer verifier post-quantum. Hash
  based transparent proofs (STARKs) have a post-quantum conjecture under hash
  assumptions and no trusted setup (Ben-Sasson et al., 2018).
- **Why keep Groth16 at all.** As a measured lower bound on size and verify
  time (three group elements, Groth 2016), so the cost of the post-quantum
  path is visible. It is a laboratory number, not a deployment option.
- **Why the bitmap is public.** Accountability: a verifier learns who is
  claimed to have signed, and a conflict names the keys involved. Hiding the
  signers (blind voting, receipt-free voting) was rejected for the base: it
  makes weight and equivocation unverifiable, and PoCI rests on attributable
  evidence.
- **Why not "1 KB".** A 1 KiB target was discussed in the technical note. It
  may be reachable for a classical proof alone; it is not demonstrated for a
  post-quantum proof of this relation, which the note says should only be
  fixed after a reproducible prototype. This OBIP leaves the targets open.
- **Alternative: change the vote signature.** A hash-based signature chosen
  to be cheap to prove (instead of in-circuit Falcon-1024 and ML-DSA-65
  verification) could cut prover cost substantially. It changes the KES leaf
  and the attestation format, so it would be its own Consensus OBIP. OPEN:
  whether to pursue it.
- **Alternative: sample a committee and certify only it.** Rejected for the
  base (random multi-round recomposition, local committee change): a sample
  trades the guarantee over all the weight for a probability, and each new
  draw is another chance for an attacker (technical note, "Troca de
  votantes"; 1,000 seats at p = 25 % give about 1.7 × 10^-9 per draw before
  the union bound over draws).

## Backwards Compatibility

No consensus rule changes. Nodes that do not implement this OBIP are
unaffected; they keep serving full certificates. Activation is a release
that ships the RPC and the verifier; no height is needed. GnB is unaffected.

## Test Vectors

Pending. Required before Proposed: statement and `statement_id` encodings,
bitmap edge cases (n = 1, n multiple of 8, n not a multiple of 8, non-zero
padding bits), quorum edges on public values (exactly two thirds vs just
above, u128 extremes), decode rejections, and proofs that must be refused
(wrong `config_id`, wrong link, bitmap not matching the witness, duplicate key,
forged weight, unknown `vk_digest`).

## Security Considerations

- **Soundness becomes trust.** Whoever accepts a compact certificate trusts
  the proof system's soundness, the circuit's correctness against the base
  rules, and the integrity of `vk_digest`. A circuit bug is a finality
  forgery for every compact-certificate consumer, while full nodes stay safe.
  The circuit needs its own review against the base reference
  (`reference/poci_reference.py` (branch `obenblock/o11a-poci-reference`)).
- **Post-quantum status.** Pairing-based SNARKs, BLS and Groth16 are not
  post-quantum. STARK-family proofs are conjectured post-quantum under their
  hash assumptions and are typically tens to hundreds of kB. For scale, one
  hybrid ML-DSA-65 + Falcon-1024 signature is about 4.6 kB (one ML-DSA-65
  signature alone is 3,309 bytes, FIPS 204).
- **Trusted configuration.** The proof says nothing about whether
  `config_id` is the right configuration. A verifier with a wrong trust root
  accepts a certificate of a fabricated configuration. Lineage is out of
  scope here (OBIP-3).
- **Safety bounds are the base's.** A valid compact certificate proves what
  a valid full certificate proves, no more: with more than a third of the
  configuration's weight signing both sides, two conflicting certificates
  exist (`DESIGN-obenblock-poci.md` §3, §11).
- **DoS.** Verification must be bounded before the proof is read: size cap,
  accepted `(proof_system, vk_digest)` pairs, cheap public checks first.
  OPEN: verify-time budget per certificate on reference hardware.
- **Data availability.** A compact certificate does not make vote data
  available. Evidence still requires full nodes that keep the votes.

## Deployment prerequisites

- A reproducible prototype of the relation on both laboratory tracks
  (`0x0001` baseline, `0x0002` hash-based), with published measurements:
  proof size, full compact-certificate size, prover time and memory,
  verify time p50/p95/p99 on stated hardware, for several `K` (technical
  note, "Medição").
- OPEN: size, prover-time and verify-time targets, set from those
  measurements.
- An independent verifier implementation and the vectors above.
- The three-lens review of OBIP-1.
